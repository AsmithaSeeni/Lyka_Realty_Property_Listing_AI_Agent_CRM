# Freehold Ownership and Eligibility

General information only. This project has NOT verified a current
list of freehold areas or buyer eligibility rules. Never name an
"approved area" list from memory.

## Short spoken explanation (Tanglish)
"Dubai-la ellaa property-um ellaa buyer-um freely vaanga
mudiyadhu nga. Area-va poruthu, buyer-oda status-a poruthu
rules maarum. Andha current list naan memory-la irundhu solla
maatten - ungalukku specific-a irukkura property-ku DLD-la
confirm pannanum. Adhu dhaan correct-aana vazhi."

## What is verified
- DLD is the authority for land and property registration in
  Dubai, and its site publishes information for owners, tenants,
  developers, real estate companies and financial institutions.
- Whether a specific unit can be owned outright, and by whom,
  depends on the rules that apply to that unit and that buyer. That
  must be checked against DLD for the specific property.

## What is NOT verified in this project
- Any list of areas where foreign nationals may own property
  outright.
- Any nationality, residency or visa condition attached to buying.
- Whether a purchase carries any residency consequence.

Treat every one of these as unverified and route the customer to
DLD or a qualified professional. Do not fill the gap with a
confident-sounding answer.

## Common misconceptions and limits
- "In Dubai anyone can buy anything anywhere." Not safe to say.
- "Buying property automatically gives you a residence visa."
  Never state this. Residency consequences must be confirmed with
  the authorities, not with a property catalogue.
- "The developer will confirm the ownership rule." They may, but
  DLD is the authority.

## Questions the customer may ask next
- "Naan foreigner, naan vaangalaama?" - explain that it depends on
  the specific property and the current rules, and offer to have it
  verified rather than guessing.
- "Visa kidaikkuma?" - say this must be confirmed with the
  authorities and cannot be promised here.
- "Which areas are open?" - do not list areas from memory; point to
  DLD.

## General versus case-specific
General: that ownership rights vary and DLD is the authority.
Case-specific: the exact status of a named unit and buyer, which
requires an official check.
