# Dubai Land Department (DLD) and RERA

General information only. Nothing here is legal advice.

## Short spoken explanation (Tanglish)
"Dubai-la property registration and rules ellam Dubai Land
Department, DLD, paakkum nga. RERA-vaanga DLD-oda real estate
regulatory wing. So licence, project registration, escrow,
complaints - ellam DLD system-la dhaan poganum. Naan edhu solla
poreno, adhu DLD website-la confirm panna mudiyum."

## What is verified
- Dubai Land Department (DLD) is the government department for
  land and property matters in Dubai, and its website is the
  official source for its services and regulations.
- The Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA) is part of DLD. DLD's
  own published material describes RERA as the regulatory arm of
  DLD, and DLD's website carries a RERA section.
- DLD's Rules & Regulations section publishes real estate
  legislation and circulars, grouped into areas that include
  anti-money-laundering material, jointly-owned property,
  valuation, leasing brokerage agreement forms and licensing
  circulars.
- DLD publishes research, indexes and real estate data, and a
  Development Handbook, in its Open Data section.

## Common misconceptions and limits
- That a broker's or developer's claim is automatically a DLD
  rule. Only DLD's own material is authoritative.
- That RERA is a separate company. It is part of DLD.
- That rules never change. Circulars and legislation are updated,
  so anything quoted from memory can be out of date.

## Questions the customer may ask next
- "Unlicensed broker-ah irundha enna panradhu?" - advise verifying
  the licence through DLD's verification service and, if the
  customer believes there is a violation, going through DLD's own
  complaint channel.
- "Rules enga paakalaam?" - point to DLD's Rules & Regulations
  page; do not paraphrase a rule from memory.
- "Escrow DLD-oda control-la irukka?" - see the escrow topic.

## General versus case-specific
General: what DLD and RERA are, and that DLD is the authority.
Case-specific: whether a particular action breaks a rule, what
penalty applies, or how a specific dispute will be decided.
