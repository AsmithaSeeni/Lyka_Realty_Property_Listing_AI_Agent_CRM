# Service Charges, Transaction Fees and Ownership Costs

General information only. This project has NOT verified any
current fee amount or percentage. Never quote a fee from memory.

## Short spoken explanation (Tanglish)
"Property vaanginadhu kku aprom, service charges-nu oru
recurring cost irukkum nga - building maintenance, common area,
adhu maadhiri. Adhu DLD jointly-owned property rules-la varum.
Transfer-ku fee irukkum, aana adhu evlo-nu naan memory-la irundhu
solla maatten - adha DLD or developer confirm pannanum."

## What is verified
- DLD's Rules & Regulations section publishes jointly-owned
  property material, including circulars about the mechanism for
  collecting service fees and about the regulation of fees for
  services or use fees related to jointly owned real estate. So
  service charges are a regulated, DLD-referenced topic.
- DLD also publishes a real estate valuation guide in the same
  section.

## What is NOT verified in this project
- Any transaction fee, registration fee or transfer fee amount or
  percentage.
- Any annual service charge amount or per-square-foot rate.
- Any agent commission rule.
- Any cost of utilities, furniture or management.

Say these must be confirmed from DLD, the developer or the
building's own budget documents.

## Common misconceptions and limits
- That service charges stop being payable at some point, or that
  they are optional.
- That service charges are the same in every building.
- That a low headline price means a low total cost of ownership.
- That a fee percentage heard from someone else is current.
- That a service-charge dispute is a matter for the seller. It is
  a building matter, and DLD's circulars describe a mechanism
  preceding the Rental Disputes Center.

## Questions the customer may ask next
- "Service charge evlo?" - unverified; get it from the building's
  actual budget or the developer.
- "Transfer fee evlo?" - unverified; confirm with DLD.
- "Idhu rent-ku kudukkuravanga-ku apply aaguma?" - service charges
  normally sit with the owner in a jointly-owned building; confirm
  the specific arrangement rather than asserting it.

## General versus case-specific
General: that recurring service charges and one-off transaction
costs exist, and that DLD regulates jointly-owned property fees.
Case-specific: the actual amounts for a named unit.
