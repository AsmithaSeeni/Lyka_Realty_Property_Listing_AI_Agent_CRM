# Rental Yield, ROI, Cash Flow and Investment Risk

Estimates only. Never promise a return, and never compute a return
from our unverified catalogue prices.

## Short spoken explanation (Tanglish)
"Rental yield-na simple-ah, oru varushathu rent-a property
price-oda divide pannuvaanga. Adhu gross. Net-na, service
charges, maintenance, vacancy, agent fee, ellam kalichu
micham-ndhu paakkanum. Idhu ellam estimate dhaan nga - guarantee
illa. Market maarum, so naan 'idhu kandippa kidaikkum'-nu solla
maatten."

## The method, explained simply
- Gross rental yield: annual rent divided by the property price,
  times 100. It ignores all costs.
- Net return: what is left after service charges, maintenance,
  management fees, agent fees and periods without a tenant.
- Cash flow: the actual money in and out each year, not just a
  percentage.
Present these as a way of thinking, never as a forecast.

## What is verified
- DLD publishes research, indexes and real estate data in its Open
  Data section, which is where a customer should look for market
  data rather than relying on a claim made on a call.

## What is NOT verified in this project
- Any current rental figure, yield, occupancy rate or price trend.
- Any projected return for any area or property.
- The current asking price of any catalogue record. The catalogue
  currency is UAE dirhams (AED), but a listed price is not a
  confirmed current price.

Never use a catalogue price to calculate ROI, and never turn a
listed price into a promised return or a guaranteed rental yield.
Never repeat a return figure that came from a brochure as if it
were fact.

## Common misconceptions and limits
- That a brochure yield is guaranteed income. It is a projection.
- That gross yield is the return the owner keeps. Costs reduce it.
- That a property is always tenanted.
- That past price growth predicts future growth.
- That a high yield area is automatically a good investment: it
  usually reflects higher risk, weaker demand or higher costs.

## Questions the customer may ask next
- "ROI guarantee-ah?" - no. Explain gross versus net, then say
  clearly that no return can be guaranteed and that any projection
  is an estimate.
- "Best area edhu?" - do not rank areas from memory; offer to
  explain the trade-offs and point to DLD's published data.
- "Rent eppo kidaikkum?" - depends on handover and the rental
  market; not verifiable here.

## General versus case-specific
General: how yield and net return are calculated, and that risk
exists.
Case-specific: the return on a named unit, which needs current
market data and the unit's real costs.
